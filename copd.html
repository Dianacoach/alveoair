Understanding COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

Causes, breathing techniques, oxygen use, and when to seek help

WHAT IS COPD?

  • Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
  • Progressive disease that limits airflow
  • Causes difficulty breathing and reduced oxygen exchange
  • Common symptoms: SOB, cough, mucus, fatigue

TYPES OF COPD

  • Emphysema: Damaged air sacs, air trapping
  • Chronic Bronchitis: Inflammation with mucus production
  • Asthma-related COPD: Long-term airway damage

CAUSES

  • Smoking (main cause)
  • Secondhand smoke
  • Pollution and occupational exposure
  • Genetic factors (alpha-1 deficiency)

BREATHING & DAILY MANAGEMENT

  • Pursed-lip breathing (slow exhale)
  • Diaphragmatic (abdominal) breathing
  • Never hold your breath
  • Exhale during effort (standing, lifting)

POSITIONING

  • Lean forward slightly
  • Support arms (tripod position)
  • Move slowly and plan activity

OXYGEN USE

  • Target saturation: 88–92%
  • Common use: 1–2 L/min (individualized)
  • Avoid excessive oxygen unless needed
  • Risk of CO₂ retention with high oxygen

IMPORTANT

  • In emergencies, oxygen is NOT restricted
  • High oxygen may be required to save life

⚠️ WARNING SIGNS

  • Severe shortness of breath at rest
  • Rapid breathing or inability to speak full sentences
  • Chest tightness or pain
  • Blue lips or fingertips (cyanosis)
  • Confusion or unusual drowsiness
  • Increased mucus or color change (yellow/green)
  • Fever or signs of infection

WHEN TO CALL FOR HELP

  • Breathing suddenly worsens
  • Oxygen is not improving symptoms
  • Patient becomes difficult to wake
  • Signs of CO₂ retention (sleepy, confused)
  • Any doubt—better to seek help early

EMERGENCY

  • Call emergency services immediately
  • Use oxygen as needed
  • Start CPR if patient is unresponsive and not breathing
