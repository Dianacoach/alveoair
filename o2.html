Oxygen Therapy & Safety Guidelines

Proper use, benefits, risks and precautions for safe oxygen use at home

O₂ Tank Duration










USES & BENEFITS

  • Provides additional oxygen above normal room air (21%)
  • Improves oxygen levels in the blood and vital organs
  • Reduces shortness of breath (SOB)
  • Supports patients with COPD, lung disease and heart conditions
  • Helps during recovery from illness, surgery or respiratory distress
  • Can improve sleep and daily activity tolerance
  • Used during exercise when oxygen demand increases
  • Essential in emergency care, CPR and resuscitation
  • Delivered through nasal cannula, mask or tracheostomy collar
  • Can be provided by oxygen tanks or concentrators
  • Higher flows may be needed during activity or illness
  • Tracheostomy oxygen requires humidification
  • Helps maintain brain and organ oxygenation
  • Can improve endurance and quality of life
  • Important support for home care patients and caregivers

RISKS & PRECAUTIONS

  • Excess oxygen in COPD may cause CO₂ retention
  • Oxygen should be adjusted based on saturation (not fixed liters)
  • Typical COPD target saturation: 88–92%
  • High oxygen levels can reduce breathing drive in some patients
  • Prolonged high oxygen may cause oxygen toxicity
  • May lead to shallow breathing and poor lung expansion
  • Increases risk of mucus retention and pneumonia if not breathing deeply
  • Not a drug, but patients may become psychologically dependent
  • Oxygen supports combustion, serious fire hazard
  • Never smoke or allow flames near oxygen equipment
  • Can cause severe burns to face and airway if exposed to sparks or cigarettes
  • Requires proper storage and handling of oxygen tanks
  • Dry oxygen may irritate airways. Humidification may be needed.
  • Patients must continue deep breathing and movement
  • Improper use can worsen respiratory condition

IMPORTANT NOTES

  • In emergencies, oxygen should NOT be withheld or limited
  • High concentration oxygen (even 100%) may be necessary to save life
  • “No more than 2 liters” is not a strict rule. Individualized care is required
  • Always follow professional guidance when adjusting oxygen levels
